Aerie

A fabrication tracker for FRC robot parts. Every part a team designs goes from CAD review through CNC or hand fabrication to done, so the shop always knows what to make next and who has it.

Live demo Code on GitHub

The problem

An FRC team designs and builds a new robot in a few weeks, and a robot is a few hundred custom parts. During build season, parts get cut on a CNC router or made by hand by whoever is in the shop that day, and the state of each one tends to live in a spreadsheet, a group chat, or someone's head.

That breaks down in predictable ways. A part gets cut from a CAD revision that changed an hour ago. Two people make the same bracket. Nobody can say what's still missing before the robot can be assembled. And students on the shop floor have to dig through CAD to find out what a part even looks like.

What I built

I built Aerie for FRC Team 3322's shop, starting in November 2025, and wrote nearly all of its 165 commits.

  • A fixed workflow. Every part moves through Review, then CNC or Hand Fab, then Completed, with the student who has it, its subsystem and material. Live search filters across all of them.
  • 3D previews from STEP files. An uploaded STEP file is converted to GLB on the server with cascadio. The browser renders eight views of it with Three.js once and uploads them, so every later visit shows images instead of loading a 3D model.
  • Onshape integration. Parts link back to their Onshape documents, and Aerie pulls drawing PDFs through the Onshape API, so the drawing at the machine matches the current design.
  • Easy for a team to run. Flask and SQLAlchemy on SQLite or PostgreSQL, an interactive setup script, and API-key authentication. A git-based updater in the settings pulls new versions, runs migrations, rebuilds the frontend and restarts the server.

Where it stands

The statistics page shows how the shop is doing: completed and in-progress counts, average completion time, and how many parts sit at each stage. A public demo runs with sample parts.
